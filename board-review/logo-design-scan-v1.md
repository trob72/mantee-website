## Purpose

The current prototype uses a temporary `M` mark. The next useful step is to decide what type of mark fits the first launch:

- A restrained text-first official mark.
- A local landmark mark.
- A Trace/crossroads identity direction.
- A Depot/heritage identity direction.
- A formal municipal seal direction.

The first launch does not need a permanent municipal seal. A readable website mark is enough if the board is not ready to approve formal logo policy.

## Observed Patterns

1. The strongest civic systems separate the website wordmark from any formal seal.
2. A logo needs to work in small header space before it needs to look impressive on a board packet.
3. Small-town websites often overuse badges and seals, which can reduce readability on mobile.
4. A simple landmark mark can feel more local than a generic seal while still remaining official.
5. If a formal seal is desired, it should be handled as a separate approval and policy item.

## Concept Directions

### Concept A: Text-First Official

Simple monogram plus wordmark.

Best for:

- Low-risk launch.
- Header readability.
- Easy WordPress implementation.
- Future replacement if an official seal is approved later.

Risk:

- Less distinctive than a local landmark mark.

### Concept B: Water Tower Landmark

Simplified water tower mark paired with official wordmark.

Best for:

- Strong Mantee-specific identity.
- Current photo direction.
- Social profile icon and website header use.

Risk:

- Needs board comfort with using the water tower as a Town symbol.

### Concept C: Trace + Crossroads

Road/crossroads/landscape-inspired mark.

Best for:

- Local story.
- Potential "Epicenter of the Natchez Trace" identity if verified.

Risk:

- Historical wording and claim need confirmation.

### Concept D: Depot + Heritage

Depot-inspired mark.

Best for:

- Community/heritage sections.
- Museum, Depot, and history materials.

Risk:

- May tilt too much toward community/history and not enough toward official Town operations.

### Concept E: Formal Seal Direction

Traditional circular seal.

Best for:

- Long-term formal identity if the board wants one.

Risk:

- Highest approval burden.
- Easy to make visually cluttered.
- Should not be treated as official without policy and final vector artwork.

## Recommendation

For launch, narrow to:

1. **Concept A: Text-First Official** if the board wants the safest immediate path.
2. **Concept B: Water Tower Landmark** if the board wants a distinct Mantee identity from day one.

Hold Concept E until the Town wants a formal seal process.

Use Concept D as a possible section badge for Depot, museum, or history content.

Use Concept C only after confirming the Trace wording and history.

## Board Review Prompt

```text
Please review these as visual directions only, not final logos.

1. Which direction feels most appropriate for official Town use?
2. Should the first launch use a text-first mark or a water tower landmark mark?
3. Is the water tower appropriate as a public-facing Town symbol?
4. Should Trace/crossroads wording be explored, or held until historical confirmation?
5. Does the Town want a formal seal later, separate from the website launch logo?
```
